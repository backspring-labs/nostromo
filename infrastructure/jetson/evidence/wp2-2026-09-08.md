```text
PASS  serve tailnet-only: serve on, funnel off
PASS  certificate: issuer=C=US, O=Let's Encrypt, CN=YE1 notAfter=Dec  8 01:48:23 2026 GMT
PASS  NIP-11 relay info            (https, from the Mac)
PASS  well-known nostr.json: {"names":{},"relays":{}}
PASS  wss upgrade 101: HTTP/1.1 101 Switching Protocols
PASS  spark NIP-11                 (https, from the Spark)
PASS  tailnet plaintext refused: http://100.98.252.95:3000/
PASS  LAN plaintext refused: http://192.168.1.23:3000/
PASS  LAN https refused: https://192.168.1.23/
```

Finding worth keeping: the relay is multi-tenant and binds every WebSocket to a community by the request `Host` header, which it derives from `RELAY_URL` at startup. A handshake with any other host, including the bare IP, gets a generic 404 `relay: no community is configured for this host`. Every client, `buzz-acp` included, must therefore connect by the exact relay hostname.

The bootstrap `RELAY_URL` with `:3000` had created a community row `nano.tailc69e7d.ts.net:3000`, which is why the first upgrade through Serve (Host without the port) was refused, and the `wss://` URL then created a second row. Confirmed in Postgres: two `communities` rows, the placeholder owner in each, two roster events, zero channels. Since nothing of value existed, the runbook's reset was applied: stop, remove the four data volumes, start. After the reset: one community (`nano.tailc69e7d.ts.net`), one owner row (the placeholder), one event, relay `self` unchanged, all 14 probes pass. The same reset is the procedure for the hostname switch (README, "Hostname switch before WP-5").
